How an ATS reads your CV

An applicant tracking system converts your CV into plain text, splits it into fields such as name, experience, education and skills, then ranks or filters candidates against the job. Recruiters search that database by job title, skill and location.

Two things go wrong. The parser can misread the layout, putting your job titles under skills or dropping a whole column. Or it reads everything correctly but your CV uses different words from the job description, so you rank low in searches.

What makes a CV maker ATS friendly

Many CV tools are built to look good on screen, not to parse cleanly. Before you choose one, check that it gives you:

01

A single-column layout. Sidebars and multi-column grids are the most common cause of scrambled parsing.

02

Standard section headings. "Work experience", "Education" and "Skills" map directly to ATS fields. Creative labels often don't.

03

Real, selectable text. Skill bars, icons, charts and text inside images are invisible to the parser.

04

Contact details in the body. Some systems skip page headers and footers, so your phone and email should sit in the main text.

05

A clean PDF or Word export. Check by copying all the text from your exported file into a plain text editor. If it comes out in the right order, an ATS can read it.

The tool is half the job

A clean layout gets your CV read correctly. Ranking well depends on content. For each application:

  • Use the exact job title from the posting where it honestly matches your role.
  • Mirror the skills and tools named in the job description, in the same wording.
  • Write achievements with numbers: budgets managed, team size, revenue, time saved.
  • Keep dates in one consistent format, month and year.
  • Name the file clearly, for example Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf.

Common questions

What is an ATS friendly CV?

A CV with a simple single-column structure, standard section headings, real text instead of images, and the terms used in the job description, so applicant tracking software can read it correctly.

Can a CV maker guarantee my CV will pass every ATS?

No. Employers configure their systems differently. A good CV maker gives you a clean, parseable structure; the content and keywords still need to match the role.
